01 — The short answerIf you only read one screen

Three of these are very good trips. One is a genuinely great one. Which is "right" comes down to a single question: is this group here for the golf, or for the week?

⏰
Deal with this first: every quote has either expired or is about to.

Cancún and Puerto Vallarta both say "pricing effective until July 26, 2026" — already past. The Dominican quote is good until August 9, 2026. Before you compare another number, email Mariana/Diego and get all four re-validated at your real headcount. The prices below are the negotiating baseline, not a guarantee.

Recommended — best all-round

Cancún / Riviera Maya — $2,259 pp

It wins the tiebreakers that actually decide whether a group trip is fun. Daily nonstops from Calgary on three airlines — the only destination here where a missed connection or a sold-out day isn't a crisis. Three completely different, genuinely good courses including El Camaleón at Mayakoba, which hosted the PGA Tour for 16 years. All-inclusive Riu Palace so nobody's chasing a bar tab. And the deepest non-golf menu of the four by a distance — cenotes, Tulum, Isla Mujeres, Cozumel diving, Fifth Avenue.

Pick this if the group has mixed handicaps, a couple of guys who'd rather snorkel than play 54, and anyone flying in from a second city.

The splurge — best pure golf

Casa de Campo, DR — $2,869 pp

This is the only option where the golf itself is the reason to go. Teeth of the Dog is a top-60 course in the world and has been the Caribbean's #1 for fifty years — seven holes carved into the rock right on the ocean. Add Dye Fore's 300-foot canyon holes and The Links, all Pete Dye, all on one 7,000-acre property. Casa de Campo is a golf resort that happens to have a beach, not the other way around.

Pick this if the group are golfers first and will happily eat a red-eye and a two-hour van ride for a course they'll talk about for twenty years. Budget an extra ~$200 pp for mandatory caddies.

Best value / most social

Costa Rica — Tamarindo Diria, $2,072 pp

Cheapest on the sheet, includes a sunset catamaran, and the only itinerary with a fully refundable cancellation policy to 30 days out — which for a 16-man group is worth real money. Tamarindo's bars and restaurants are a walk from the lobby. Two caveats, both real: you play Reserva Conchal twice, and late February is peak Papagayo wind season in Guanacaste — 30–50 mph is normal, gusts go higher. Confirm whether the Diria rate includes meals; if it doesn't, the price advantage largely evaporates.

Shortest flight, best town

Puerto Vallarta — $2,389 pp

The easiest travel day of the four — 4h50m nonstop, three carriers, and the airport is fifteen minutes from the hotel. February is peak humpback whale season in Banderas Bay, and the Zona Romántica restaurant scene beats everything else here. But you're paying $130 more than Cancún for a course lineup most golfers would rank below it, and the Vidanta round requires the Ameca bridge crossing. Great week; slightly soft golf value.

03 — Getting thereNonstop from Calgary, Toronto & Houston

All four destinations have a nonstop from all three cities in February — but the three groups will have wildly different travel days. Calgary is the constrained one: two of its four routes hang on a single WestJet flight with limited frequency, and the Tuesday arrival the quotes assume may not line up. Houston golfers, by contrast, fly roughly half as long and pay roughly half as much.

🌎
Three things that only show up once you add Toronto and Houston.

1. Toronto can fly nonstop into La Romana (LRM) — the airport inside Casa de Campo, ten minutes from your room. That removes the two-hour van transfer that is the biggest strike against the Dominican option, but only for the Toronto contingent. 2. February is the most expensive month of the year out of both Canadian cities and one of the cheapest out of Houston — Texans aren't fleeing winter, so they're not bidding the fares up. 3. Houston's flight times are in a different class: 2h20m to Cancún and 3h34m to Liberia, against 5h20m and 6h50m from Calgary.

The old Calgary detail

Kept below for the Calgary contingent, who have the tightest constraints.

🥇 Cancún (CUN) — the safe bet

  • WestJet — year-round, roughly daily
  • Air Canada — seasonal, from December
  • Flair — seasonal, from December
  • ~7 departures/week · 5h20m · 4,088 km
  • Economy, premium economy and business all sold
Why it matters

Three carriers and a daily departure means the group can arrive on different flights, book at different times, and still all get there. It's the only destination here with real redundancy if something goes sideways.

🥈 Puerto Vallarta (PVR) — the easiest day

  • WestJet — year-round
  • Air Canada — from December
  • Flair — from October
  • ~6 departures/week · 4h50m · 3,468 km
  • Shortest flight of the four by ~2 hours
Why it matters

Leave Calgary mid-morning, be on a lounger by mid-afternoon. Add a 15-minute airport transfer and it's the only option where Tuesday is a usable half-day rather than a write-off.

🥉 Costa Rica — Liberia (LIR)

  • WestJet only — seasonal winter service
  • Up to ~5×/week in peak season
  • 6h50m · frequently an overnight (e.g. 23:35 → 06:25)
  • Air Canada flies LIR from Vancouver, not Calgary
  • LIR → Tamarindo/Conchal ≈ 60–75 min
⚠️
Tuesday risk.

Published YYC–LIR service clusters Wednesday through Sunday. If there's no Tuesday departure in the Feb 2027 schedule you'll need to shift the itinerary a day or fly Monday and eat a night. Verify before deposits.

Dominican Republic — Punta Cana (PUJ)

  • WestJet only — ~3×/week, seasonal
  • 6h52m · departs ~00:05–01:05, arrives ~09:00
  • Boeing 737 MAX 8 · economy + premium only
  • La Romana (LRM) is 10 min from Casa de Campo but has no Calgary nonstop — Air Transat serves it from Toronto and Montreal only

The real cost of the DR.

A midnight red-eye, a 9am landing, then two hours in a Sprinter. You'll arrive tired. Worth it for Teeth of the Dog — but tell the group in advance so nobody books a Tuesday afternoon tee time.

Read the Punta Cana number with some caution.

KAYAK puts February and April as the two most expensive months on YYC–PUJ at C$1,538 and C$1,438 — which fits a single carrier flying three times a week into peak Caribbean season. Cheapflights, working from a different data set, shows a much gentler route-wide average of C$794. The honest answer is that this route is volatile and thinly served, so the spread between a good booking and a bad one is far wider than on the Mexico routes. Budget high and book early, or shop it hard.

✈️
The surprise: Costa Rica has the cheapest flight, not the most expensive.

At a C$703 February average, Liberia comes in roughly C$125 under Cancún and C$195 under Puerto Vallarta — despite being the longest of the three and served by a single airline. WestJet appears to be pricing LIR to fill a seasonal route. That materially strengthens Costa Rica's value case and it's the single biggest change to the maths on this page.

Booking notes for a group this size

  • Book flights before you pay the land deposit. Three of these four itineraries assume a Tuesday arrival that only Cancún and Puerto Vallarta can comfortably deliver.
  • WestJet group bookings (10+) hold seats with a smaller deposit and later name deadlines than individual fares — worth a call for a 12–16 man block on the single-carrier routes.
  • Watch the clubs. WestJet and Air Canada both treat golf bags as standard checked baggage within weight limits; Flair charges for everything. On a 5-hour flight the cheap fare stops being cheap once 16 sets of sticks are aboard.
  • Frequency = leverage. On CUN and PVR you can shop three airlines. On LIR and PUJ, WestJet knows it's the only game in town and February is peak.

The Riviera Maya, off the course

When you're not playing

🏛 Ruins & cenotes

Tulum's cliffside ruins sit right above the Caribbean — an hour away. Chichén Itzá is a full day but it's one of the New Seven Wonders. In between, Dos Ojos and Gran Cenote let you swim through underground limestone caverns.

🎣 Fishing & diving

February is prime sailfish season off the Yucatán. Charter a boat for the Saturday. Or take the ferry to Cozumel and dive Palancar Reef — part of the second-largest barrier reef on earth, with 30m+ visibility.

⛵ Isla Mujeres day

The classic group catamaran: open bar, snorkel stop at the reef, lunch on the island, and Playa Norte — routinely voted one of the best beaches in the world. The easiest "everyone's happy" day on this list.

🌴 Eco-parks

Xcaret, Xel-Há and Xplor — underground river swims, zip-line circuits, amphibious vehicles. Xplor in particular is a very good hangover cure for a group that overdid Thursday.

🍸 Fifth Avenue

Playa del Carmen's Quinta Avenida is a kilometres-long pedestrian strip of restaurants, rooftop bars and mezcalerías. Coco Bongo if the group wants a spectacle. All of it walkable from the Riu.

⛳ Bonus 18

PGA Riviera Maya, Moon Palace and Playa Mujeres are all within reach if anyone wants a fourth round on the Saturday. No other destination here gives you that many spare options.

Verdict

The most bookable trip on the sheet, and it doesn't compromise much to get there. Cheapest of the three all-inclusive options, best flight access by a wide margin, three genuinely distinct courses with a real headliner, and enough off-course variety that a 12–16 man group with different appetites all gets a good week. If the group can't reach consensus, this is the one nobody will be unhappy about.

Banderas Bay, off the course

When you're not playing

🐋 Humpback whales

February is the peak. Banderas Bay is one of the largest humpback breeding grounds in the Pacific and you'll see breaching from the boat, from the beach, sometimes from the tee box. This is the single best "wow" moment available across all four destinations and it's free with almost any boat trip.

🏝 Marietas Islands

A protected marine park with the famous Hidden Beach — a collapsed crater you swim into through a sea tunnel. Access is permit-limited so book early. Snorkelling here is the best on Mexico's Pacific coast.

🍽 Zona Romántica

The best restaurant scene of the four, comfortably. Old-town cobblestones, rooftop dining over the Malecón, world-class seafood and a serious mezcal culture. If the group are eaters, this is your destination.

🎣 Sportfishing

Banderas Bay produces sailfish, dorado and roosterfish year-round, with the deep water close to shore so you're fishing within 30 minutes of the marina. Half-day charters work well around a golf schedule.

🏄 Sayulita & San Pancho

45 minutes north — surf towns with beach bars, taco stands and a completely different energy from the resort strip. Easy half-day with a driver.

🥃 Tequila country

You're in Jalisco. Distillery tours in the agave fields, or the shorter Vallarta-based tastings. A very easy sell to a golf group on the Saturday.

Verdict

The best week, arguably — shortest flight, best food, whales in the bay, a proper town instead of a resort strip. But it's $130 more than Cancún for a course lineup most golfers would rank a notch below, and Vista Vallarta is doing a lot of the heavy lifting. If the trip is really a guys' week that happens to include golf, this is a strong pick. If it's a golf trip, Cancún gives you more for less.

💵
Budget for caddies — they're not in the quote.

Caddies are compulsory on Teeth of the Dog and Dye Fore. Expect roughly $25–30 in caddie fee plus a customary $25–40 tip, per player, per round. Across the week that's realistically $150–250 per golfer on top of the $2,869. Not a dealbreaker — but tell everyone up front and bring small US bills.

Where you stay

Casa de Campo — Junior Suite, double occupancy, all-inclusive

A 7,000-acre gated estate, not a hotel — the courses, the marina, the beach, the shooting range and the village are all on property, connected by golf cart. Every guest gets a cart, which for a group of 16 turns the whole resort into your playground. Junior suites are big and recently refreshed. The all-inclusive plan covers meals, drinks and most activities. This is a different category of property from the Rius — genuine Forbes-rated luxury with a fifty-year golf legacy attached.

The estate, off the course

When you're not playing

🔫 The Shooting Center

The sleeper hit for golf groups. 245 acres of sporting clays, trap and skeet across more than 200 stations. Nothing else on this page produces the same amount of trash talk. One round of 25 cartridges is typically included in the AI plan.

🏛 Altos de Chavón

A full-scale replica 16th-century Mediterranean village on the cliff above the river, with cobblestone streets, artisan studios, galleries, restaurants and a 5,000-seat Grecian amphitheatre. Genuinely worth the hour.

⛵ Saona & Catalina

Catamaran day trips to two of the Caribbean's postcard islands. Catalina has excellent diving and a wall drop-off; Saona is the classic palm-and-sandbar beach day with a rum bar on the boat.

🎣 Marlin fishing

The Marina Casa de Campo fleet fishes blue marlin, mahi and wahoo. The marina itself is the best dining precinct on the property — worth an evening even if nobody fishes.

🏇 Polo & horses

Casa de Campo has a full equestrian centre and active polo fields. Lessons, trail rides along the river, or just watch a chukka with a drink.

🏖 Minitas Beach

A private crescent with a beach club, snorkelling, kayaks and paddleboards. Calm water, good food, and quiet — the recovery day after 54 holes.

Verdict

If the group's answer to "why are we going" is golf, this is the trip. It's ~$600 more per person than Cancún, plus caddies, plus the worst travel day of the four — a midnight red-eye and a two-hour drive. What you get for that is Teeth of the Dog, which is a legitimately world-top-100 golf course, and a resort that has been built around golfers since 1971. The trade-off is that Casa de Campo is a bubble: it's magnificent inside the gates and there is not much reason to leave them. For a group that wants a big night out somewhere new, that's a genuine minus.

Reserva Conchal (repeat)
Robert Trent Jones II · 8:00 AM · catamaran 2–6 PM

You play Conchal a second time, then go straight to a sunset catamaran cruise. Playing a course twice isn't a disaster — you'll score better the second time, and there's a case for finishing on the best track in the region. But it is the weakest golf variety of the four options, and unlike every other shortcoming on this page, this one can't be negotiated away.

⛳
Why the repeat is structural, not sloppy quoting.

Guanacaste has exactly three championship courses: Hacienda Pinilla, Reserva Conchal, and the Arnold Palmer Ocean Course at Peninsula Papagayo. The third is the problem — play there is restricted to guests staying on the Papagayo peninsula (Four Seasons or Andaz), with proof of stay required. Booking from Tamarindo Diria or the Westin doesn't get you on. So a three-round Guanacaste package staying anywhere else has only two courses available, and one of them has to repeat. Asking the agent to swap in a third distinct course won't work — the courses don't exist. It's the price of the destination, and it's worth knowing before you compare this against Cancún's three or Casa de Campo's three.

📄
One thing to confirm in writing anyway.

The itinerary's overview line names Reserva Conchal for Feb 27, but the detailed daily schedule table for that day just says "Golf & Catamaran, Tee Time 8:00 AM" and never names a course. Given the access situation above it's almost certainly Conchal again — but get it stated explicitly so nobody arrives expecting something new.

💨
The Papagayo winds are the risk you have to price in.

Guanacaste in late February is spectacular for weather — essentially zero rain, 30–35°C, endless sun. It is also the heart of the Papagayo wind season, when offshore gusts routinely hit 30–50 mph on the Pacific coast. On exposed, links-style courses like Hacienda Pinilla and Conchal that isn't a breeze, it's a different game. Some groups love it. A group of mid-handicappers shooting 20 over their handicap for three straight days will not.

Guanacaste, off the course

When you're not playing

🌋 Hacienda Guachipelín

The best single non-golf day on this entire page. At the base of Rincón de la Vieja volcano: canopy zip-lines, horseback riding, volcanic mud baths and natural thermal hot springs, all in one adventure-park day. Perfect use of Friday's open slot.

⛵ Sunset catamaran

Already included, Saturday 2–6 PM. Open bar, snorkel stop, and the Pacific sunset off Tamarindo as the finale to the week. No other quote bundles an activity like this.

🏖 Playa Conchal & Flamingo

Conchal's beach is made of billions of crushed shells rather than sand — genuinely unusual. Flamingo next door is white sand and calm water, and the base for most boat charters.

🎣 Roosterfish & sails

Guanacaste is one of the great sportfishing coasts on earth and February is prime — sailfish, marlin, and the roosterfish this coast is famous for. Charters leave from Flamingo and Tamarindo.

🐒 Wildlife

Palo Verde and the Tempisque River boat safaris put howler monkeys, crocodiles, iguanas and scarlet macaws within a couple of metres. Costa Rica's real differentiator versus the other three.

🏄 Tamarindo town

Surf lessons in the morning, ceviche and cold Imperial in the afternoon, and a bar scene that goes late. If you stay at the Diria it's all on your doorstep.

Verdict

The best-designed itinerary of the four — a rest day, a catamaran, an adventure day, and the only cancellation policy that doesn't punish a group whose numbers shift. At $2,072 it's also the cheapest, and it has the cheapest flight. What holds it back is the golf, and the ceiling is fixed: the region only has two courses you can actually book from these hotels, so one gets played twice — on a coast where late-February wind can genuinely ruin the scoring. If the group's priority is a great week at the best price, this wins. If it's 54 holes of varied golf, it can't get there no matter how the package is rewritten. Get the meal plan clarified and it's very hard to beat on value.

Hidden costs, ranked by how much they'll surprise you

  • Casa de Campo caddies — $150–250 pp. Mandatory on two of three rounds. The single biggest unlisted cost on this page.
  • Tamarindo Diria meal plan — up to $750 pp. If that rate is room-only, five days of food and drink for 16 people is a five-figure line item that isn't in the proposal. Ask today.
  • Costa Rica SUV rentals. The proposal recommends two large SUVs to make the open day work — roughly $600–900 for a few days, split across the group, plus fuel and Costa Rica's mandatory liability insurance which is charged separately and always surprises people.
  • Golf bag fees. Free within weight limits on WestJet and Air Canada; charged on Flair. Sixteen sets adds up fast on a "cheap" fare.
  • Group size re-pricing. Mexico was quoted at 12, DR and Costa Rica at 16. Per-person cost on the Mexico quotes will likely drop at 16 — get both re-quoted at your actual number before comparing.
  • Tips and cart fees. Budget $20–30 per round for forecaddies and bag staff in Mexico and Costa Rica even where not required.

07 — Next stepsWhat to do this week

In order. The first two are time-sensitive.

01

Re-validate all four quotes — today

Cancún and Puerto Vallarta expired July 26. The DR quote dies August 9. Email Mariana Recines (mrecines@golfinmexico.net) and Diego Castellanos (diego@golfinmexico.net) and ask for all three Best4Golf quotes re-issued at your actual headcount, with the Mexico pair repriced at 16 rather than 12.

02

Lock the headcount

You have quotes at two different group sizes, which makes the comparison unfair to Mexico. Twelve versus sixteen changes per-person pricing, van sizes, and whether you get a private tee-time block. Get a firm number before anything else.

03

Check that Tuesday flight actually exists

Pull up Feb 23, 2027 on WestJet for LIR and PUJ specifically. If there's no Tuesday nonstop, those two itineraries need reshaping — and that may resolve the decision for you.

04

Ask the three questions that change the ranking

(a) Does the Tamarindo Diria rate include meals and drinks? (b) What is Casa de Campo's all-in caddie cost per player per round, in writing? (c) Confirm which course Costa Rica plays on Feb 27 — the detail table never names it. Note that (c) is a confirmation, not a negotiation: with only two bookable courses in Guanacaste, a repeat is unavoidable there. Either of the first two could move a destination up or down a full tier.

05

Take a straw poll on one question

Not "where do you want to go" — you'll get four answers. Ask instead: "Would you pay $600 more and take a red-eye to play a top-100 course in the world?" If the group says yes, book the Dominican. If it hesitates, book Cancún. That's genuinely the whole decision.

06

Then move fast on flights — and count the cities first

February is peak season out of both Calgary and Toronto (though not Houston), and 12–16 seats on the same aircraft is not a small ask. Get a head count by departure city before you decide: if a real share is coming from Toronto, the Dominican Republic's biggest drawback disappears — they land at La Romana, ten minutes from the resort, and the premium over Cancún drops from about C$1,845 a head (Calgary) to roughly C$1,150. Once the destination is settled, book the air before the land deposit clears; the flight is the constraint, not the hotel.
