Which suite is worth the money

What you get for each

  • Junior Suite — around 430 sq ft, separate sitting area, balcony, king or two doubles. The standard room at every Riu Palace; it is not a downgrade of anything.
  • Courtyard View — the same room looking inward instead of at the garden. Ten dollars.
  • Swim-up Elite Club — ground floor, terrace opening onto a shared shallow pool, plus Elite Club membership: reserved beach cabanas, à la carte breakfast, priority restaurant reservations, a lounge.

What travellers consistently say

Traveller forums and review threads are remarkably consistent on swim-up rooms across Riu and comparable all-inclusives, and the themes cut against the upgrade for a group like ours:

  • No privacy. A long-running traveller thread on swim-up suites in this region makes the point plainly: the terrace sits a few feet from the pool edge, anyone in the water can see the patio, and they can see into the room if the curtains are open.
  • Damp rooms. The same discussion flags that swim-up rooms sit against the water and can smell musty.
  • The reliable win is loungers, not the pool. The consistent positive is never having to fight for a chair — your own loungers are outside the door.
  • Elite Club, separately, gets good reviews. A detailed Riu Elite Club write-up from a guest who researched the same question concluded the beach cabana access alone made it worth paying for, with faster drink service and better restaurant tables; the lounge itself went unused.

Reddit threads specific to Riu Palace México and its swim-up category are thin — the sentiment above comes from traveller forums and Riu Elite Club reviews rather than one Reddit post, so treat it as the general consensus on the room type rather than a verdict on this exact property.

Recommendation: Junior Suite for everyone, with Elite Club as an individual opt-in

We are tee-ing off at 9:00am three days out of five. The private pool is a feature you use in the late afternoon, at a resort that already has four pools and a beach. Paying $420 a head — $5,040 across the group — for a ground-floor terrace onto a shared pool is the weakest value per dollar in the quote.

The part of the upgrade that genuinely earns its keep is Elite Club: cabanas, better breakfast, priority reservations. If a few guys want that, ask Mariana whether Elite Club is available on a standard Junior Suite without the swim-up, and whether individuals can upgrade rather than the whole block. That is the question to put to her before we vote.

Courtyard View at +$10: take it or leave it. Ask which one actually gets us six adjacent suites — keeping the group on one corridor is worth more than the view.

Four things to check before we commit

  • The resort is not in Riviera Cancun. Riu Palace México is in Playacar, Playa del Carmen — around 50 minutes south of Cancún airport. Fine for golf (all three courses are close), but the quote title is misleading and it changes transfer time.
  • The 12:00pm transfer times are fixed in the quote. Everyone's flights need to land in a window that works for one van, or we pay for a second. Worth collecting arrival times before anyone books.
  • Pricing expires 26 July 2026. That has passed. Get the quote re-issued before we circulate numbers for a vote.
  • Cancellation tightens fast. Deposit-only penalty up to 60 days out; 50% at 59–45 days; 75% at 44–15 days; 100% inside 14 days. Golf alone is refundable up to 72 hours before play, less $25 per player. With twelve people, one or two will drop — decide now whether we buy insurance or self-insure.
